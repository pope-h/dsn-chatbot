## Who DSN is
- Data Science Nigeria (DSN) describes itself as Africa's foremost AI and Data Science
  organisation, focused on talent development and sector-specific AI solutions.
- Legally registered as the Data Scientists Network Foundation (non-profit). Also goes by
  "Data Scientists Network" / DSNai.
- Founded in 2016 by Olubayo (Bayo) Adekanmbi, who is the Founder and Lead Mentor. Sources
  differ on the exact month. In 2026 DSN is marking ten years ("DSN@10", #DSNat10).
- Mission: raise 1 million AI talents in Nigeria/Africa within ten years, and build AI
  solutions that improve the lives of 2 billion people in emerging markets.
- Work areas: research, product development, training and community building, consulting,
  and incubating AI startups.
- Scale (dated): about five years in, more than 500,000 people had gone through its free
  training programmes. The AI Bootcamp 2026 page cites 10,000+ participants.
- Website: https://datasciencenigeria.org

## DSN communities and cities

  Clubs), **Community of Professionals**, **Ladies in AI**, and the AI+ communities for
  kids and teens.

## Programmes and platforms
- **AI Bootcamp**: annual, all-expenses-paid, residential/hybrid bootcamp on advanced
  machine learning and deep learning. Highly competitive, multi-stage selection.
- **AI Everyday**: year-round introductory track delivered as six-week intensive sprints,
  for complete beginners.
- **AI Invasion**: free beginner AI classes, held "in every city". Open to everyone;
  registration is required and gives an AI+ Membership ID. Topics include intro to machine
  learning, regression and classification, clustering and sampling.
- **AI+ for Kids and Teens**: communities for school students. Kids: about ages 7–11
  (Grades 4–8). Teens: about ages 12–18 (Junior Secondary 3 to Senior Secondary 3).
- **AI+ Clubs / Campus Communities**: groups for university students to build AI skills
  beyond their courses.
- **Ladies in AI** and **Arewa Ladies4Tech**: programmes for women. Arewa Ladies4Tech
  focuses on empowering women in Northern Nigeria with AI and digital skills.
- **AI on Demand / Data Scientist on Demand**: talent recruitment and client AI work.
- **AI Startup Lab**: incubating AI startups. **Coworking space** and **Corporate
  Training** are also listed.
- **DSN x Microsoft Elevate**: AI for Future Workforce and AI Developers training.
- **Competitions** on Zindi and Kaggle, plus research, academic research and white papers.
- **Consulting** services.

## Data science basics
- **Data science** is the practice of getting useful knowledge from data. It combines
  statistics, programming, domain knowledge and communication. Typical goals: describe what
  happened, explain why, predict what will happen, and recommend what to do.
- **Artificial intelligence (AI)** is the broad aim of making machines do tasks that need
  intelligence. **Machine learning (ML)** is the part of AI where systems learn patterns
  from data instead of following hand-written rules. **Deep learning** is ML using
  many-layered neural networks. Data science uses ML as one of its tools, but also
  includes data cleaning, analysis, visualisation and communication.
- **Data analytics** mostly answers questions about the past and present (dashboards,
  reports). **Data science** adds prediction and experiments. **Data engineering** builds
  the pipelines that collect and store data. **MLOps** is running ML models reliably in
  production.
- Types of data: **structured** (tables, spreadsheets, databases), **unstructured** (text,
  images, audio, video), and **semi-structured** (JSON, logs). Variables are **numerical**
  (continuous or discrete) or **categorical** (nominal or ordinal).
- Key statistics ideas: mean, median and mode; variance and standard deviation;
  distributions (normal, skewed); correlation (two things move together, which is not
  the same as one causing the other); sampling and bias; probability; hypothesis tests and
  p-values; confidence intervals.
- A simple example: predicting house prices from size, location and age is a regression
  problem. Deciding whether an email is spam is a classification problem.

## The data science process
1. **Define the problem**: what decision will this help with, and how will success be
   measured?
2. **Collect data**: databases, APIs, surveys, files, sensors, web data.
3. **Clean and prepare data**: handle missing values, duplicates, wrong types and
   outliers; combine tables. This often takes most of the project time.
4. **Explore (EDA)**: summary statistics and charts to understand patterns and problems.
5. **Feature engineering**: create inputs that help the model (for example, day of week
   from a date).
6. **Model**: choose and train a model on training data.
7. **Evaluate**: test on data the model has not seen, using the right metric.
8. **Deploy and monitor**: put it to use and watch for drift as the world changes.
9. **Communicate**: explain results clearly to non-technical people.
- Common mistakes: data leakage (information from the future or from the test set leaks
  into training), overfitting, using the wrong metric, ignoring class imbalance, and
  trusting biased data.

## Machine learning
- **Supervised learning**: learn from labelled examples. *Regression* predicts a number;
  *classification* predicts a category.
- **Unsupervised learning**: find structure without labels, for example clustering
  (grouping similar customers) and dimensionality reduction (PCA).
- **Reinforcement learning**: an agent learns by trial and reward.
- Common algorithms: linear and logistic regression, decision trees, random forests,
  gradient boosting (XGBoost, LightGBM), k-nearest neighbours, support vector machines,
  k-means clustering, and neural networks.
- **Train/validation/test split**: train on one part, tune on another, and report final
  performance on a test set that was never used for training. **Cross-validation** repeats
  this across different splits.
- **Overfitting**: the model memorises the training data and does badly on new data.
  **Underfitting**: it is too simple to capture the pattern. Fixes include more data,
  simpler models, regularisation and early stopping.
- **Metrics**: for regression, MAE, RMSE and R². For classification, accuracy, precision,
  recall, F1 and ROC-AUC. Accuracy can mislead when classes are imbalanced (for example,
  99% of transactions are not fraud). Choose the metric that matches the real cost of
  mistakes.
- **Bias-variance trade-off**: simple models have high bias; very flexible models have
  high variance. Good models balance the two.

## Deep learning and generative AI
- **Neural networks** are layers of simple units that learn weights from data using
  backpropagation and gradient descent. Deep learning excels at images (CNNs), sequences
  and language (transformers).
- **Computer vision** (image classification, object detection), **natural language
  processing (NLP)** (translation, sentiment, summarising) and **speech** are major
  application areas. Frameworks: PyTorch, TensorFlow and Keras.
- **Large language models (LLMs)**, such as Gemini, are transformer models trained on huge
  amounts of text to predict the next word. They can answer questions, summarise,
  translate and write code, but they can state false things confidently ("hallucinate").
- **Prompting** is how you instruct an LLM. **Grounding** means giving the model trusted
  facts and telling it to answer only from them. **RAG (retrieval-augmented generation)**
  searches a document collection and gives the model only the relevant pieces. **Fine-
  tuning** continues training a model on your own data. **Embeddings** are numbers that
  capture meaning, used for semantic search.
- **Responsible AI**: watch for bias in data and models, protect privacy, be transparent
  about limits, keep a human in the loop for high-stakes decisions, and test fairness
  across groups. Low-resource African languages (Hausa, Igbo, Yoruba, Pidgin) are
  under-served, which is why DSN runs LLM benchmarking tracks for them.

## Tools and skills
- **Python** is the most common language for data science. Core libraries: NumPy, pandas
  (tables), Matplotlib and Seaborn (charts), scikit-learn (classic ML), PyTorch and
  TensorFlow (deep learning), Jupyter notebooks and Google Colab (free to start).
- **SQL** for getting data out of databases. **R** is popular in statistics and research.
- **Spreadsheets and BI tools**: Excel, Power BI, Tableau, Looker Studio.
- **Geospatial**: QGIS, ArcGIS and GeoPandas for maps and spatial data.
- **Big data and cloud**: Spark, Azure, AWS, Google Cloud.
- **Version control**: Git and GitHub for tracking and sharing code.
- **Where to practise**: Kaggle (datasets, notebooks, competitions), Zindi (African data
  challenges), DSN hackathons and free classes.
- **Soft skills** matter as much: asking good questions, storytelling with data, and
  working with people who know the domain.

## Careers and learning path
- Roles: data analyst, data scientist, machine learning engineer, data engineer, AI
  engineer, business intelligence developer, research scientist, and AI product manager.
- A sensible beginner path: (1) learn Python basics; (2) learn pandas and basic
  statistics; (3) learn to make charts and tell a story; (4) learn SQL; (5) learn
  supervised machine learning with scikit-learn; (6) do 2–3 real projects with public
  data; (7) move to deep learning or a specialty (NLP, vision, geospatial); (8) share
  your work on GitHub and Kaggle.
- Projects that impress: predicting something useful with Nigerian or African data,
  dashboards that answer a real business question, and a deployed app people can try.
- Tips: build a portfolio, join a community for feedback and accountability, find a mentor,
  and keep learning in small steady steps rather than long gaps.
